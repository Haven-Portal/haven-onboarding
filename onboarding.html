Role Overview & Team Structure

Welcome & What to Expect
Role context, team structure, and work hours
View details

Welcome to the Outbound SDR team! This role is fast-paced and dynamic full cycle sales role. You'll spend the first two weeks meeting with team members and learning about the company and the role.

Team Structure

  • You are part of the Outbound SDR team (7 reps).
  • You will be paired with an Inbound SDR — each Inbounder supports two outbound reps.

Work Hours

  • 9–5:30 EST with 30 minutes for lunch.

What We Sell

  • Advertising placements in Haven Lifestyles Real Estate magazines.
  • Each market produces 8 issues per year.
  • Each issue runs on a 6-week cycle.
  • At times issues can overlap where you are working two of the same market simultaneously: opening a new one and closing a previous one.

Sales Cycle & Weekly Workflow

Monday Workflow & Send Cadence
What happens each week across a 6-week slate
View details

Every Monday You Will:

  1. Start a new market edition — mail merge emails go to agents with active listings.
  2. Continue closing the prior edition: secure commitments, collect ad materials, get approvals, push deals to "Approved" status.
  3. Expect high inbound volume on Mondays.

Send Cadence (across 6 weeks)

WeekWhat Happens
Week 1Mon: First email to active listings. Wed: RE follow-up (usually includes a better offer). Agents who respond are removed from future sends.
Week 2Quiet week for this slate — no sends.
Week 3Tue: First email to new listings (came on market after Week 1). Thu: RE to new listings. Also: Contracts & Subscriptions (ASU), 1/6 pages, and CWO (cover wrap offer) sent.
Week 4Third RE — very short, deadline-driven ("checking in before we close the issue"). Also: Past Issue Advertisers (PALI) email sent once magazines are physically delivered. Focus: "Did you receive your copies?" + renewal.

Interested Folder Follow-Up Training

The Decision Framework
What to do with each lead in your Interested folder
View details

Every lead in your Interested folder falls into one of these scenarios. Work through each one systematically:

1. No Text Sent Yet

Action: Send a text. If the lead has received multiple emails but no text has been sent, text immediately. Every lead must be texted at least once within the first 5 business days.

2. 5+ Emails Sent, No Text

Action: Text immediately. Email alone isn't enough — text creates urgency and visibility.

3. 5 Emails + Already Texted

Now evaluate timing:

Last TouchAction
1–3 days agoSkip for now — too soon.
~1 week agoSend the "Last-Ditch Email" (see template below).

4. Lead Responded Previously

Match their communication channel. If they responded via email → follow up via email. If they engaged via text → follow up via text.

5. Dead Lead Criteria

A lead is considered dead when they have received 5+ email follow-ups, 1 manual follow-up, and at least 1 text with no response. At this point: stop prioritizing.

The Follow-Up Formula
Every message — text or email — must include all four elements
View details
ElementWhat It Looks Like
1. IntroRemind them who you are. "Hi [Name], just following up…"
2. Recap OfferBe specific. "We discussed a full page at $295…"
3. Value PropositionGive a reason to act — exposure, audience, or event tie-in. "This issue aligns with [event] and will reach [audience]…"
4. Clear Close (Question)Every message must end with a question. "Should I hold a page for you?" / "Want me to get a layout started?"

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • No closing question — kills conversion every time.
  • Vague messaging — "Just checking in" is weak and signals low effort.
  • Not recapping the offer — forces the prospect to do work they won't do.
  • Over-templated / robotic tone — signals you're not paying attention to them.
  • Ignoring prior conversation — makes you look careless.

Outbound Process & Expectations

Primary Outbound Lists
What to work first, every day
View details

Outbound is not cold calling — it is relationship-based prospecting built on prior engagement, data, and timing. It represents at least 50% of your role.

A. Daily Salesforce New Listings List — Highest Priority

  • Updates every morning; includes past advertisers with new listings, filtered by market and listing date.
  • Listings over $1M must be 100% reviewed. Work down to $750K then $500K as time allows.
  • "Worked" means contacted — or reviewed and staged appropriately.
  • Not every lead will be contacted, but every lead must have eyes on it.
  • Leadership monitors via last stage-change date and coverage percentage by month.

B. PALI List (Past Advertiser Last Issue) — Weekly

  • Delivered once per week; includes advertisers from the most recent issue (excludes anyone in a current deal).
  • High conversion rate — work within the first few days of receiving it.
  • Ideal for reruns, quick renewals, and subscription conversations.

C. Secondary Sources

  • Past issue "Interested" and "NLI" folders and prior unresponsive conversations.
  • Best practice: maintain a personal system (folder, tag, or doc) for warm prospects.

Daily Expectations & Outbound Sprints
Call minimums, sprint structure, and follow-up timing
View details

Daily Call Minimums

  • Minimum: 30 calls/day
  • Target: 50 calls/day
  • Half day: Hit 15 minimum before logging off

Outbound Sprints

  • Two scheduled sprints per day (rotating AM/PM).
  • One sprint per day is all-hands expected; second is encouraged but flexible.
  • A proper sprint includes: working lists and prioritizing hot leads, calls and texts, real-time stage updates, clearing or reclassifying leads.
  • Outbound time is for execution and movement, not admin.

Outreach Channel Standards

  • Calls: Mandatory first step of every outreach. Voicemails are optional, not required.
  • Texting: Approved primary follow-up channel. Keep texts short, relevant to the listing, and clear on next steps.
  • The goal is efficient contact, not maximum touchpoints.

Follow-Up Timing

Must be context-driven, not arbitrary. Base timing on what the agent said, the listing lifecycle, and prior conversations. Example: "next month" → follow up closer to that time; "no for this issue" → try to understand why.

Call Training & Scripts

Mindset, Tone & Value Propositions
Core philosophy before you pick up the phone
View details

Core Philosophy

  • The goal is a smooth "yes," not a combative win.
  • Realtors often push back — do not escalate.
  • The sale should generally be simple and easy.
  • Focus on making it easy for them to say yes.

Tone Guidelines

  • Acknowledge objections. De-escalate, do not challenge.
  • Never speak poorly about other marketing methods.
  • Assume interest rather than asking if they're interested.

Two Primary Value Propositions

  • Exposure: Print + Digital reach of 15,000 readers per issue.
  • Tangible Tool: Listing presentations, client gifting, office display, brand reinforcement.
When agents say "everyone already knows me" or "I only get word of mouth" or "I don't need exposure" — pivot from exposure to tool and branding value.

Rebuttals & Common Objections

Handling Common Objections
What to say when agents push back
View rebuttals

"It's Too Expensive / I Don't Have the Budget"

  • Break down the cost per reader (a few cents per reader).
  • Recap the value points.
  • Suggest splitting cost across multiple listings, and highlight branding value.
  • Frame as an investment with ROI potential, not money down the drain.
  • Offer delayed billing.

"I Don't Do Print Anymore"

  • Explain the online exposure: 15k+ readers online per issue, social media posts, landing page, editorial article.
  • Acknowledge the main value can be online, while still explaining print benefits (client gift, listing presentations, price reduction leverage).
  • If still adamant: offer 100% online for $150, which includes the full online package.

"Listing Should Be Going Under Contract Soon"

  • Agents feature sold/under contract listings all the time to attract new listings.
  • Great way to appeal to potential sellers and show recent success.
  • Still gets branding value and can serve future listing presentations.
  • Backup in case the sale doesn't go through.

"I've Never Heard of Haven"

  • Start by explaining you consistently work with top brokerages and agents in their market.
  • Name a few agents at their brokerage once you know them.
  • Walk through the value proposition.
  • "This is why we can offer an introductory rate."

"The Season Is Slow"

  • 15,000 readers either way, regardless of season.
  • Lean on any theme or event tied to the issue.
  • Point out where the season will be when the issue actually comes out — often agents don't account for the timing lag.
  • Limited investment during slow season keeps you in front of readers while costs are low.

"I Don't Have Time"

  • We handle all of the design — virtually no work for them.
  • If pushback: we can pull photos from the MLS/online, so they don't need to send anything.

"Last Time I Didn't Get Any Leads"

  • Put things in perspective: how many times did they advertise? Was it once or twice, during the slower season?
  • Was their client happy? Did it help on listing presentations?
  • We can't track people who see the listing and then go to the agent's site directly.
  • Ask what they're seeing elsewhere for comparison.

"I Have a New Listing Coming On I'd Rather Feature"

  • Offer a 2-issue deal: feature current listing this time, new listing next month.
  • Potentially offer BOGO — next issue free once the new listing comes on.

"Not at This Time / Maybe in the Future"

  • Offer to follow up closer to the deadline (6–4 weeks out from print date).
